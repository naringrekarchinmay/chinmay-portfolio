Noted

F1 stewarding, explained. Named after the race control message "incident noted". Noted reads every FIA document from a race weekend and answers the question fans actually ask: why this penalty, and was it consistent with the last time this happened.

GitHub Repo

Every F1 weekend the FIA publishes dozens of PDFs: stewards' decisions, summonses, technical delegate reports, the race director's notes. Fans argue about the penalties for days, and almost nobody reads the documents or the regulations behind them. Noted reads all of it and gives you the ruling, the rule, and the precedent in one place.

It is built as a free, local system: rule-based parsing for the fields that rules can handle, a local model through Ollama for the few narrow steps that need language, SQLite for storage, and a static site for reading. Nothing leaves the machine, and every published claim can be traced back to a line in the original document.

The hard part is not extraction, it is judgment: deciding when two incidents are really alike, and writing a plain-English brief that never says more than the document does. Noted treats both as problems to measure, not to hand-wave. Precedent is scored against hand-checked incidents, and every brief is linted against its source before it is shown.

Unofficial fan project. It uses public FIA documents and open timing data and is not affiliated with Formula 1 or the FIA.

Highlights

What it does.

📄

Reads the rulings

Fetches the FIA listings, classifies each PDF by title, and parses stewards' rulings into fields, keeping each claim's position in the source so it points back to a line in the original.

🧾

Local model, quote checked

For narrative documents a local model (gemma4:e4b via Ollama) fills the fields rules cannot, with a quote check that drops anything it cannot find in the text. On the gold set it invented zero quotes.

🔁

Precedent with a challenge

TF-IDF over the stewards' wording with names masked, plus rare-factor overlap, then a local-model challenge that re-rates the top candidates for on-track incidents. Fifteen of twenty test incidents pass a blind check.

✍️

Briefs that cannot drift

The model writes two or three sentences; a lint rejects any number, name, or quote not in the document, any statement of the decision, verdict words, and filler, and sends the problems back for a rewrite, up to four drafts.

🧑‍⚖️

You be the steward

Each ruling page hides the decision until you rule on it yourself, kept in your browser, then reveals the stewards' reasons with cited quotes, the precedents on one penalty scale, and a replay of the cars.

📘

Linked to the rulebook

The regulations are split into articles (379 in Sporting Regulations Section B, plus the code of driving conduct), and an article reference in a 2026 ruling links straight to the article it cites.

Results

How it performs.

Extraction on a gold set of 30 rulings, labelled before any model ran. gemma4:e4b was chosen over qwen3.5:9b for its precision, its honesty on quotes, and half the time per ruling.

Outcome and penalties are handled mostly by rules; the model is used for the narrative documents that have no decision field. The open problem is factor precision (0.58): about four in ten factors the model reports carry a real quote that does not actually support the label, and a per-factor check pass is the next step. On precedent, adding the challenge loop lifts the pass rate to 15 of 20 incidents (mean relevance 1.37 of 2). On briefs, a blind check of 20 read 13 correct, 6 minor, and 1 wrong, with no role swaps; across the full run the lint passed 988 of 1,151 briefs within four drafts and hid the rest.

Approach

How it was built.

01

Fetch and classify

Pull the FIA document listings for a race, download the ruling PDFs, and classify each one by title.

02

Parse into fields

Turn each PDF into text and structured fields (session, car, driver, alleged breach, article, facts, decision, penalty, reasoning), keeping each field's position in the source.

03

Local-model extraction

Run the narrative documents through gemma4:e4b on Ollama with a fixed vocabulary and a quote check, scored against a 30-ruling gold set.

04

Precedent and its challenge

Rank earlier rulings by TF-IDF over the stewards' wording with names masked and rare-factor overlap, then let the local model challenge and re-rank the top candidates for on-track incidents.

05

The notice board

Build a static site where each weekend is a board of rulings by session, each ruling hides its decision until you rule on it, and 324 rulings since 2023 carry an OpenF1 replay of the cars.

06

Briefs and the copy desk

Write a two or three sentence brief per ruling and lint it against the source, rejecting anything the document does not say, with the decision and the precedent comparison composed in code.

07

Rules

Split the current rulebooks into articles and link each 2026 ruling's article references to the article it cites, or to the FIA PDF when the rulebook is missing.
