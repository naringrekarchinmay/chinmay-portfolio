Staten Island Railway OTP Forecasting

An end-to-end, explainable machine learning system that forecasts Staten Island Railway on-time performance from real MTA Open Data, validates it against statistical baselines, and says how confident each forecast should be.

GitHub Repo

Most transit reporting looks backward: it tells you what on-time performance was. This project asks what OTP will be, why the model thinks so, and how confident it should be.

Built entirely on MTA Open Data for the Staten Island Railway, the project runs through a ten-notebook pipeline: historical trend analysis, machine-learning prediction, recursive multi-month forecasting, SHAP-based explainability, and operational risk scoring. The model is benchmarked against naive, moving-average, SARIMA, and Prophet baselines and validated with chronological cross-validation. Residual-based prediction intervals give each forecast an error range, and a six-page Streamlit dashboard, styled for an executive audience, delivers the results.

It connects directly to my day job in transit operations analytics: turning raw reliability data into forward-looking, decision-ready insight.

Highlights

What it does.

📊

Six-page dashboard

Home, System Health, OTP Trends, AI Forecast, Scenario Lab, and Research pages give leadership the state of the railway at a glance.

🥊

Baseline benchmarking

XGBoost goes head-to-head against naive, moving-average, SARIMA, and Prophet baselines, and beats the strongest of them by about 18.7% in forecast error.

🔮

Recursive forecasting

A multi-month forecasting engine feeds each prediction back as a lag feature, projecting OTP 3–12 months ahead.

📏

Calibrated uncertainty

Residual-based prediction intervals achieve 79.5% and 89.7% actual coverage against 80% and 90% targets, so each forecast carries a calibrated error range.

🚦

Operational risk scoring

Forecasted months are classified Low / Medium / High risk (≥95%, 90–95%, <90% OTP) with a plain-language outlook message.

🧠

Explainable AI with SHAP

SHAP values reveal the drivers behind each prediction such as seasonality, delay rate, recent OTP momentum, and rolling trends.

Approach

How it was built.

01

Data loading & review

Ingest Staten Island Railway OTP data from the MTA Open Data portal and audit quality and coverage.

02

Cleaning & EDA

Standardize the series, then explore seasonality, delay patterns, and long-run reliability trends.

03

Feature engineering

Build time-series features: OTP and delayed-train lags, rolling averages, delay rate, year, month, quarter, season, and day-time category.

04

Model training & selection

Benchmark Linear Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost. The race is close: Linear Regression posts the lowest MAE, while XGBoost leads on RMSE and R² and is carried forward as the main forecasting model.

05

Explainability

Apply SHAP to the final model so every forecast can answer not just “what?” but “why?”.

06

Recursive forecasting

Project OTP 3–12 months ahead by feeding each prediction back as a lag feature, with Low / Medium / High risk scoring.

07

Baselines & validation

Benchmark against naive, moving-average, SARIMA, and Prophet baselines, then stress-test with 5-fold chronological time-series cross-validation.

08

Uncertainty & dashboard

Add residual-based prediction intervals, document the research methodology, and ship everything as a six-page Streamlit dashboard.
