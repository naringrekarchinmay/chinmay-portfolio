Disease Progression & Multimorbidity Prediction

A leakage-aware machine learning pipeline that predicts hospitalization, ICU admission, 30-day readmission, and multimorbidity progression from longitudinal EHR-style data, with explainability built in from the start.

GitHub Repo

Can a patient's demographics, baseline diagnoses, lab history, medications, and prior hospitalizations predict what happens to them next? This project puts that question to the test on synthetic electronic health records spanning 2018–2024, built from five linked tables: patients, diagnoses, lab results, medications, and outcomes.

The most important design choice is a strict temporal cutoff. Predictors are built only from events on or before December 31, 2021; outcomes are measured from 2022 through 2024. Patient-level diagnosis flags are deliberately excluded, and baseline conditions are rebuilt from dated diagnosis events before the cutoff, so the model can't peek at the future it is asked to predict.

The philosophy is the same as my transit work: explainability and decision-support storytelling. A risk score nobody can interpret doesn't change a clinical decision; a transparent one might. It connects directly to my doctoral research in information systems engineering management, where decision-support systems meet real operational and clinical environments.

Highlights

What it does.

🏥

Hospitalization risk

Predicts which patients will be hospitalized in the 2022–2024 outcome window from their pre-2022 history.

🚨

ICU admission risk

Estimates each patient's risk of an ICU admission during the outcome window.

🔁

30-day readmission

Models the risk of readmission within 30 days, a core hospital quality and cost metric.

🧬

Multimorbidity progression

Among patients with fewer than two baseline conditions, predicts who progresses into multimorbidity.

⏳

Leakage-aware design

A strict temporal cutoff separates predictors (through 2021) from outcomes (2022–2024), the same split a deployed model would face.

🧠

Explainability first

Feature importance and SHAP analysis expose the drivers behind every risk score, so predictions can support clinical judgment instead of replacing it.

Approach

How it was built.

01

Data loading & cleaning

Ingest the five raw EHR tables, audit coverage and quality, and standardize them for analysis.

02

Patient profiles & multimorbidity EDA

Explore patient demographics, condition prevalence, and how chronic conditions cluster and co-occur.

03

Feature engineering

Build baseline features from dated pre-cutoff events: demographics, baseline conditions, lab history, medication history, and prior hospitalization patterns.

04

Baseline models

Establish simple, honest reference models for each prediction target before reaching for anything fancier.

05

Progression prediction models

Train gradient-boosted models (XGBoost, LightGBM) for hospitalization, ICU, readmission, and multimorbidity progression.

06

Evaluate, explain & deliver

Compare models across multiple metrics, layer SHAP explainability on top, and surface everything in a Streamlit dashboard.

At a glance

Project details.

Data source
Synthetic EHR data (2018–2024): patients, diagnoses, lab results, medications, outcomes
Problem type
Multi-target risk prediction (classification) with progression modeling
Prediction targets
Hospitalization · ICU admission · 30-day readmission · multimorbidity progression (2022–2024)
Temporal design
Predictors from events on or before Dec 31, 2021; outcomes measured Jan 1, 2022 to Dec 31, 2024
Pipeline
Ten ordered notebooks from data loading through EDA, feature engineering, modeling, evaluation, and SHAP explainability
Interface
Streamlit dashboard summarizing the processed dataset, model metrics, and generated figures
Status
Active work in progress - modeling and evaluation are still evolving
