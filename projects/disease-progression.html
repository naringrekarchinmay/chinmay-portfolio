Disease Progression & Multimorbidity Prediction

A PhD research project that begins as leakage-aware progression prediction on synthetic EHR data and turns into a harder question: given that you can predict who will progress, when is it actually worth ordering the labs to catch it, and does a smarter monitoring schedule beat a simple one?

Research branch GitHub Repo

The project started where most clinical ML projects do: predict disease progression and future hospital use from 100,000 synthetic patient records (2018 to 2024), using only events on or before a fixed 2021 cutoff so nothing from the future leaks into the features. That part works. The harder, more interesting work was deciding what the prediction is actually for.

Early on, an audit of the data turned up a problem in the dataset itself: the synthetic generator writes future diagnoses backward into past records, so a patient's pre-cutoff medications and lab flags quietly reveal conditions they are not diagnosed with until years later. Pre-cutoff creatinine alone predicts a future kidney-disease diagnosis at AUROC 0.982. None of that is leakage in the pipeline code, but a model that reads those features is cheating through the data. So the whole research line was rebuilt on a leak-free version, and the honest numbers came down, which is the point.

From there the question shifted from prediction to decision. Catching a condition early only matters if you measure the patient, and every measurement costs money and clinician time. So the project reframes monitoring as a sequential decision: each quarter, measure this patient or not, trading the price of a lab panel against the cost of leaving a condition undiagnosed. That turns a prediction score into a policy question, evaluated on a cost-grounded simulator with a pre-registered design.

The data is synthetic, so none of the clinical findings transfer to real patients. The contribution is the method and the honesty: a validated simulator, a pre-registered sweep, and results reported as they came out, including the ones that did not go the way they were expected to. Developed as part of my PhD coursework in Information Systems Engineering Management.

What makes it research

Beyond a model.

🕵️

Caught the data leaking

An audit found the synthetic generator encodes all-time diagnoses into pre-cutoff medications and lab flags. The research pipeline rebuilds the data leak-free and reports the lower, honest performance that results.

💵

Cost-grounded decision model

Monitoring is framed as a quarterly measure-or-not decision, with the price of a lab panel priced from 2026 Medicare rates against the cost of an undiagnosed condition-month.

🧪

A validated simulator

A patient simulator stands in for the policy environment and is checked against the real data with the same metric code, passing 38 of 39 validation targets within tolerance.

📝

Pre-registered evaluation

The sensitivity sweep is written down and advisor-approved before any run, disclosing what was chosen after seeing results, so the headline cannot be quietly tuned to a flattering number.

🎛️

Policy and off-policy evaluation

Policy values are computed exactly from the simulator and compared to fixed schedules, random controls, and a learned fitted-Q policy, with bootstrap confidence intervals over multiple seeds.

⚖️

An equity lens

Every schedule is checked for how it redistributes monitoring across age groups, not just for accuracy, surfacing fairness effects a prediction metric hides.

The prediction layer

What the models do.

Multimorbidity progression on the leak-free data, for the at-risk cohort of 41,654 patients who start with fewer than two conditions. Numbers are the test split at a fixed seed.

Before the generator leak was fixed, the same XGBoost scored 0.864; closing the leak dropped it to 0.828, and the deep sequence models (GRU, Transformer) do not beat a calibrated gradient-boosted tree on the honest data. The rarer, harder future-hospitalization target (4.55% positive) tops out around ROC-AUC 0.694 and is miscalibrated (Brier 0.211), so those scores are useful for ranking who to review, not as absolute risks. SHAP points to the expected drivers (prior hospitalizations, age, Charlson index), and a Cox model confirms age (hazard ratio about 1.02 per year) and comorbidity burden (about 1.49) raise the progression hazard.

The decision layer

When is it worth measuring?

Restricted mean detection delay in the simulator (all four conditions, signal strength beta = 1), with annual spend per patient. A measurement is priced at $214.49 for a full panel plus office visit.

Monitoring scheduleSpend / patient-yrMean delay (months)
Observed care$7911.9
Clinical guideline$112 to $12410.1
Fixed 12-month$2146.5 ✓
Random, same budget as 12-month$2148.0
Fixed 3-month$8583.0

Two findings the project did not expect, and reports anyway. First, at the originally planned price of an undiagnosed condition-month ($50), never measuring is the best policy at every signal strength: a measurement simply is not worth its cost below about $115 per condition-month, so the headline price was moved to $200 and the sweep extended, a change recorded in the pre-registration. Second, on the real observed visits an uncertainty-threshold controller beats budget-matched random measurement only slightly and does not beat a plain annual schedule, because closely spaced visits mostly repeat information. A fixed 12-month schedule still clearly beats random spending at the same budget, and the threshold rule shifts monitoring across age groups far more than its small accuracy gain would justify.

Approach

How it was built.

01

Leakage-aware pipeline

Build features only from events on or before the 2021 cutoff, drop all-time diagnosis columns, and enforce it with tests so no target or future information reaches the model matrix.

02

Audit the data

Discover that the synthetic generator writes future diagnoses into past medications and lab flags, rebuild a leak-free dataset, and re-run every result on it.

03

Baseline and sequence models

Train and calibrate gradient-boosted trees, then GRU and Transformer trajectory models with Monte Carlo dropout uncertainty, comparing them honestly on the leak-free data.

04

Price the measurement

Build a cost model from 2026 Medicare lab and office-visit rates, so every monitoring decision has a real dollar price on one side of the trade.

05

Simulate and validate

Stand up a patient simulator for the monitoring environment and validate it against the real data with shared metric code, passing 38 of 39 targets.

06

Pre-register, then sweep

Write down the sensitivity sweep before running it, then compute exact policy values and a learned fitted-Q policy across signal strengths and prices, with bootstrap intervals over seeds.

07

Report what held up

Measure detection delay, cost, and the age-group distribution of monitoring, and write the results as they came out, including the ones that overturned the plan.

At a glance

Project details.

What it is
A research project on disease-progression prediction that reframes monitoring as a cost-aware sequential decision
Data
Synthetic EHR for 100,000 patients, 2018 to 2024, with a 2021-12-31 cutoff separating predictors from future outcomes; an at-risk cohort of 41,654 for progression
Integrity finding
The dataset generator encodes future diagnoses into pre-cutoff records (pre-cutoff creatinine predicts future kidney disease at AUROC 0.982); the research pipeline rebuilds the data leak-free
Prediction
Leak-free multimorbidity progression at ROC-AUC 0.828 (calibrated XGBoost), with GRU and Transformer sequence models no better than the tree; the rarer hospitalization target around 0.694, used for ranking only
Decision model
A quarterly measure-or-not policy, rewarding against the price of a lab panel (about $140 to $214) versus the cost of an undiagnosed condition-month, discounted at 3% a year
Simulator
A patient simulator for the monitoring environment, validated against the real data on 38 of 39 targets
Evaluation
Exact policy values, fixed and random baselines, a learned fitted-Q policy, a masking study on real visits, bootstrap confidence intervals, and multiple seeds, all pre-registered
Honest headlines
At the planned price, never measuring wins; an uncertainty-threshold schedule beats random but not a plain annual schedule, and skews monitoring by age
Reproducibility
A reusable research package and a 27-file test suite focused on leakage prevention and simulator validity
Status
Active research, developed in PhD coursework; synthetic data only, so no clinical finding transfers to real patients
